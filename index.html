What’s a hack?

A hack is a focused session where a small team explores a real problem, tests possible solutions and builds something together toward an attainable objective. It can be technical, creative or practical. It ends with something made and a record of how it was made.

Why drumsticks[hack] is different.

A low-stakes way to meet and connect with people: you collaborate on a shared problem and build a working solution together.

An active community, not a one-off

The events are ongoing. Unlike most tech events and conferences, you don’t attend once and move on. The same community meets again and keeps growing.

Build in public, in a fun way

Every solution is documented on a public ledger: the problem, the team and the outcome. It is a record you can point to for any purpose.

Learn by solving real problems

Technology moves too fast to schedule time for all of it, especially alongside a full-time job. Here you solve problems with technologies you wouldn’t otherwise use, learning alongside peers and like minds.

Meet people by building with them

A shared problem makes for better introductions than small talk. You work side by side, see how people think, and leave with a working solution and people you would build with again.

Small by design

Each hack has at most 10 people, and we’re starting with 5. A small group means everyone contributes and is heard, and the connections you make are meaningful.

A single loop, from problem to record.

Collaboration breaks down on unclear scope, mismatched skills and open-ended commitments. The system fixes each one up front.

  1. 1

    A real problem is posted

    A host brings a problem with an attainable objective, such as a working prototype of the simplest functional solution, and lists the skills it needs.

  2. 2

    The team is formed by skill

    Applicants are screened against the roles the problem requires: backend, frontend, UI/UX, and whatever else it demands.

  3. 3

    The team builds in a session called a hack

    A time-boxed session aimed at the objective. Scope is cut to fit the time, never the other way round.

  4. 4

    The work becomes a record

    The problem, decisions, collaborators and outcome are documented and published.

A track record you can point to.

Every session produces a public entry. Over time the record becomes a body of real work, credited to the people who built it.

  • Each entry ties the problem, the team and the outcome together.
  • Contributors are credited by role.
  • Sessions are documented with write-ups and recordings.
